# Gates versus signals

A **gate** decides whether a candidate can do the job at all. A **signal** decides how well. Mixing them is the most common way a screen goes wrong: a nice-to-have becomes a silent filter, or a real must-have gets averaged away.

## The test for a gate

A requirement is a gate only if all three hold:

1. **The role cannot be done without it**, or the law or a regulator requires it. A licence to practise, a security clearance the role needs, the right to work in the country where the job is.
2. **It can be verified** from a resume, an application form, or a screening call.
3. **The JD or the hiring manager says so** explicitly. You do not promote a signal to a gate on your own judgement.

Fail any one and it is a signal.

## Common traps

| Looks like a gate | Usually is | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "5+ years of experience" | Signal | Years stand in for capability; a strong 4 beats a weak 8. Score the evidence. |
| "Degree in computer science" | Signal, or drop | Rarely required to do the work. Keep only when a regulator or the hiring manager insists. |
| "Based in Bengaluru" | Gate only for on-site or hybrid roles with stated office days | For remote roles it is not a criterion at all. |
| "Right to work in the UK" | Gate | Required by law; usually unverifiable from a resume, so it lands on the screening-call list. |
| "Fintech experience" | Signal | Domain is learnable; weight it by how much of the job depends on it. |
| "Top-tier university" | Weak signal, ≤ 5 total with other weak signals | Correlates with background more than capability. Never a gate. |
| "Immediate joiner" | Gate only if the hiring manager confirms | Unverifiable from a resume; screening-call item. |

## Unverifiable gates

Most gates cannot be confirmed from a resume: work authorisation, notice period, willingness to relocate. These are **unverifiable**, not failed. They never lower the score. They go on the candidate's screening-call list, and a candidate who would otherwise be shortlisted lands in Review until a human checks them.

A gate is **failed** only when the resume states the opposite in plain words, for example "seeking remote-only roles" against a stated on-site requirement.
